## Sunburn Management and Post-Injury Medical Considerations

**Introduction and Background**

Sunburn is an epidermal inflammatory condition resulting from prolonged
exposure to ultraviolet radiation. Individuals sustaining actinic injury
should be advised that symptomatic management constitutes the primary
component of post-exposure care, as the inflammatory process is
self-limiting in the majority of uncomplicated presentations.

**Symptomatic Management Protocol**

The application of topical emollients containing aloe vera or comparable
hydrating compounds, administered two to three times daily to the affected
cutaneous surfaces, has been demonstrated to mitigate the subjective
experience of discomfort associated with the inflammatory response while
simultaneously supporting the rehydration of compromised epithelial tissue.
Cool compresses may additionally be utilized at the discretion of the
affected individual, taking care to avoid direct application of ice to the
injured integument, as this may precipitate additional tissue compromise.

Oral hydration should be encouraged. The ingestion of generous quantities
of water over the subsequent twenty-four-hour period is recommended in
order to replace fluid loss attributable to the inflammatory process.

Analgesic agents, if utilized, should be taken strictly in accordance
with the labeled dosage instructions on the product packaging. It is
advisable that individuals consult with a pharmacist or licensed
healthcare provider prior to initiating any over-the-counter medication
regimen, particularly in the presence of comorbid conditions, concurrent
pharmacotherapy, or prior adverse medication reactions.

**Activities to avoid**

Exposure to additional solar radiation should be avoided until the
affected integument has attained complete resolution of active
inflammation. Tight-fitting garments, which may exacerbate discomfort
through mechanical friction, are contraindicated for the duration of the
acute inflammatory period. Additionally, the rupture of blisters is
discouraged, as the integrity of the vesicular membrane provides a natural
protective barrier against secondary microbial colonization of the
underlying dermal layers.

**When to seek medical attention**

Patients should be counseled to seek evaluation by a qualified medical
professional should they experience systemic symptomatology, including
but not necessarily limited to: the development of a fever, the presence
of chills, the occurrence of nausea, or the manifestation of
lightheadedness. Additionally, evidence of secondary infection — as
manifested by increasing erythema, purulent drainage, or the development
of areas of increased warmth and tenderness — warrants prompt clinical
assessment. Indications of severe dehydration, including decreased urine
output or the presence of orthostatic symptomatology, similarly represent
indications for which timely medical evaluation is appropriate.
