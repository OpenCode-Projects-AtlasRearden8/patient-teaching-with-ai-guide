## What it is

A fever is a higher-than-normal body temperature. It is usually the
body fighting an infection. It can look scary, and it is okay to call us
with questions.

## What to do

1. Take your child's temperature the way the nurse showed you, and write
   down the number and the time.
2. Give fever medicine exactly as your care team instructed, and no more
   often than the label says.
3. Offer small drinks often. Little sips count.
4. Dress your child in light clothes that feel right for the room.

## What NOT to do

- Do not give aspirin to a child. Ask your care team what to use instead.
- Do not bundle your child up or use ice or cool baths.
- Do not mix fever medicines unless your care team tells you to.

## Call your clinic if

- Your child is hard to wake up or seems "not there."
- Your child is breathing hard or fast.
- Your child will not drink, and you see fewer wet diapers or trips to
  the bathroom.
- The fever has lasted longer than your care team told you to expect.
- You see a rash that does not fade when you press on it.

## When to get help right away

Use the "call now" signs and phone numbers your care team gave you at
discharge. If your child has a seizure, seems lifeless, or you cannot
wake them, get emergency help immediately.
