# Glossary: Plain Words for the Jargon We Used

> **DISCLAIMER — EDUCATION ONLY, NEVER MEDICAL ADVICE.** This glossary defines
> workflow vocabulary used in this manual. It defines no clinical guidance.
> **Never put patient data into any AI tool.**

**AI (artificial intelligence), language model** — software that generates
text by predicting likely continuations of what you wrote. It does not
"research," does not "know" facts, and can be wrong with total confidence.
That is why every chapter treats its output as a draft.

**Clinician** — a licensed professional who assesses and treats patients
(RN, NP, PA, MD, PT...). What this manual teaches is how to *draft for* them;
it never replaces them.

**Context window** — the amount of text an AI model can "see" at once. Long
documents past it get ignored, which is why you paste a single handout, not a
folder.

**De-identified** — scrubbed of anything that could point to a specific
person: names, dates, MRNs, rooms, rare diagnoses. This manual's stricter,
simpler standard: **draft at the condition level, from memory, so nothing
identifiable ever needs scrubbing.**

**Flesch Reading Ease (FRE)** — readability score, 0–100, higher = easier.
Aim 60–80 for patient handouts; the tool's pass bar is 60+. See [03-readability-checks.md](03-readability-checks.md).

**Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL)** — the same readability idea expressed
as a U.S. school-grade number. Aim 6.0–8.0; the tool's pass bar is grade 8 or easier.

**Footer line** — the small text at the bottom of every handout declaring
education purpose, source, reviewer, and AI-assist status. Chapter 02 and
chapter 05 give the exact pattern.

**Hallucination** — AI jargon for a confident, invented statement. In
healthcare education, most show up as plausible numbers ("don't lift more
than 10 pounds for 3 weeks") or citations that do not exist.

**Local-first** — building the workflow so it prefers tools that run on your
machine and degrade gracefully offline. No text needs to leave the building,
which makes the privacy habit structural. See [01-getting-started.md](01-getting-started.md).

**PHI (protected health information)** — legally protected patient data under
privacy law (HIPAA in the U.S.): names, dates, chart contents, photos, even
"which unit." This manual's rule outranks curiosity: **no PHI enters any AI
tool, ever, for any reason.**

**Prompt** — the text you type to an AI to get a result. Good prompts here do
three things: assign a role, lock the facts, and demand questions instead of
inventions.

**Readability** — how easily text can be read, measured (in this manual) by
sentence length and word difficulty via FRE/FKGL. See
[03-readability-checks.md](03-readability-checks.md).

**Syllable** — a beat in a word. "Reading" has two; "administration" has
five. Syllable density is exactly what readability formulas measure, which is
why "use" beats "utilize."

**Teach-back** — closing the loop by asking patients to explain the plan in
their own words. It evaluates the teaching and the sheet, never the patient.
See [04-teach-back.md](04-teach-back.md).

**Version** — a dated, numbered copy of a draft (v1, v2...). The manual's rule:
if a file has no version and date in its name, it is not a controlled document;
it is a pile.
