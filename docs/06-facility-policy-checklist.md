# Facility-Policy Checklist: Clear the Handout, Not Just the Text

> **DISCLAIMER — EDUCATION ONLY, NEVER MEDICAL ADVICE.** This checklist is a
> starting framework for conversations with your facility; it is not legal or
> compliance advice, and it never overrides your actual policies. When this
> document and a policy disagree, the policy wins. **Never put patient data
> into any AI tool.**

## Why the checklist exists

The mechanics of drafting (chapters 02–05) are the easy part. The part that
protects your license and your employer is confirming that **who, what, and
where** are all inside the lines. Most AI-related incidents in workplaces trace
not to a bad draft but to a tool nobody cleared or a review step somebody
skipped. This checklist is one page; keep it with the draft.

## Copy-paste checklist

```markdown
## AI Handout Clearance Checklist (fill in before printing)

- [ ] 1. TOOL. AI tool name/version: ____
      - [ ] Cleared by facility policy (chapter 01 three-question screen)
      - [ ] Location of processing known (local / enterprise / public)
- [ ] 2. NO PATIENT DATA. Confirm nothing from a real patient's record,
      conversation, or photo entered the draft, the tool, or its transcript.
- [ ] 3. SOURCE OF TRUTH. Every clinical fact traced to:
      source title/number: ____ (facility library item or approved source)
- [ ] 4. REVIEW. Reviewed by: ____ (name, credentials) on date: ____
      Second reviewer (for new topics): ____
- [ ] 5. READABILITY. Flesch-Kincaid grade: ____ (target 6.0-8.0)
      Tool output saved at: ____
- [ ] 6. TEACH-BACK. At least one teach-back question drafted per key
      action step (chapter 04).
- [ ] 7. APPROVAL. Approved through: ____ (committee/educator/owner) on: ____
- [ ] 8. FOOTER. Sheet footer includes education-only line, source line,
      approval name/date, and the AI-assist human-review line.
- [ ] 9. VERSION. File versioned (v#) and dated; obsolete copies moved
      to archive/; printed stock replaced where applicable.
```

## The policy three-question screen, expanded

Chapter 01 introduced three questions; here is the expanded version to take to
your manager or educator, half-page, no shorthand:

- **Is AI-assisted drafting of patient-education materials permitted at all?**
  Some facilities: yes, quietly; some: yes, formally; some: no, still being
  decided. Your answer to "who decides?" determines step 7 of the checklist.
- **Which tools are approved, and under what agreement?** Enterprise tools
  governed by your facility's vendor review are different from personal
  accounts. "My home laptop with a local model" is different again, and some
  facilities disallow it for anything work-adjacent even though nothing leaves
  the machine — policy can be stricter than physics.
- **Where in the workflow is review required, and by whom?** Confirm the
  minimum second-look: for edits to existing handouts, for new topics, and
  before a draft touches paper. If nobody can answer, that is the first
  conversation to have — before the first tool session, not after.

## Two non-negotiables regardless of policy wording

1. **The PHI rule.** No patient data into any AI tool, no matter how
   the policy reads. If policy says " permitted for non-PHI tasks," the jugular
   is: keep the workflow at condition-level generality by habit, so that "no
   PHI" is structural rather than careful.
2. **The access rule.** Your access to AI tools is your facility's, not yours
   to amplify. Do not copy a facility handout into a public/tool cloud to
   "improve" it. Use the local-first patterns from chapter 01: bring your own
   words, or bring nothing.

## If your facility has no AI policy yet

That is common and fixable. Bring the three-question screen and this checklist
to your educator or compliance office as a **starting proposal** for policy —
framing it as "how nurses can safely draft clearer handouts, with mandatory
human review and no patient data" tends to open the conversation better than a
problem report does. The local-first structure of this manual (no PHI enters
any tool, no cloud, fully auditable scripts and logs) maps well onto the shape
of most privacy and oversight rules.
